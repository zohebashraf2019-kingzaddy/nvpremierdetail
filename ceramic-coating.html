Ceramic coating

A hard, glass-like layer that bonds to your paint and takes the abuse of Reno sun, Tahoe winters and hard water so your clear coat doesn’t.

Not a wax

Wax sits on top of the paint and washes away in weeks. A coating is a semi-permanent, sacrificial layer: it absorbs the damage to protect the paint underneath.

  • Lasts 3 to 6 yearsThe most durable protection you can put on paint, with proper care.
  • Deep glossA depth of shine wax can’t match.
  • Hydrophobic and self-cleaningWater beads and rolls off, taking dirt with it. Washing is faster.
  • Resists light swirlsThe kind that come from washing and drying. It doesn’t make the car invincible.

Prep is everything

A coating locks in whatever is on the paint. Skip the prep and you seal in swirls, and you can see exactly where it went on wrong.

  1. Decontaminate

    Foam bath, iron remover and clay bar strip bonded contamination.

  2. Correct & polish

    Swirls, water spots and oxidation are polished out before anything is sealed.

  3. Apply & cure

    Applied panel by panel in controlled conditions, then left to bond.

  4. Inspect

    Every panel checked under light for high spots before handover.

Common myths

Coatings are durable and glossy, but they have limits. Knowing them upfront means no surprises later.

“It makes my paint scratch-proof.”

It resists light swirls from proper washing and drying. Drag a dry, dirty mitt, a hand or a leg across the paint and it will still scratch.

“It stops rock chips.”

No layer of wax or coating can stop the impact of a rock. Only paint protection film (PPF) does that.

“Once it’s on, I never have to maintain it.”

Coatings need care to reach their lifespan. Wash and dry properly, and boost the coating periodically. A 6-year coating can start breaking down at year 3 if it’s neglected.
